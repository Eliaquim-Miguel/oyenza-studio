This release is for the Mac.

**Oyen goes black around the notch.** A MacBook's camera notch is black hardware that no app can colour, so the cream Oyen island showed it as a black hole in its top edge. Where the island wraps a notch, the Oyen theme is now black like the notch, with Oyen on it: cream text, his orange (the play button and the volume bar too), his typeface and his face. On a Mac without a notch, and on Windows, Oyen stays cream.

**Glass on the Mac.** Pick **Glass** in the menu bar's Theme menu or on the Settings page. On a Mac, Glass is macOS's own glass: Liquid Glass on macOS 26, and a dark blur on macOS 14 and 15. It's the dark kind, so white text stays readable over any wallpaper. Where the island meets the notch it keeps a black strip that fades into the glass, and when it rests beside the notch it's black, as the notch is. The glass shows when the island opens. Oyen Poni doesn't record your screen for it: macOS draws the glass itself. If Glass looks flat, turn off **Reduce transparency** in System Settings › Accessibility › Display.

**The System page reads your Mac.** On the Mac the System page showed dashes and "Reading…". It now shows your Mac's CPU (every core, with the performance and efficiency cores on Apple silicon), its graphics processor, memory the way Activity Monitor splits it (with the pressure gauge and swap), your drives, the network connection in use, the battery (charge, cycles, health, Low Power Mode) and your apps using the most, with their icons, the way Stats lays them out. The apps, drives and battery are read only while the page is open. While it's closed, a light reading once a second keeps the charts' last minute, as on Windows, and nothing is read while the screen is asleep or the Mac is locked. macOS has no public way for apps to read temperatures or clock speeds, so those show a dash.

**Install:** download `Oyen-Poni-Setup.exe` and run it. It installs just for you (no admin rights) and can start with Windows. Or unzip the portable zip and run `Poni\poni.exe`.

**On a Mac:** open `Oyen-Poni-Mac.dmg` and drag Oyen Poni into Applications. It isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so the first time macOS warns you, click **Done**, then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**.

**Upgrading from 0.1.x:** run the new installer. It upgrades in place and keeps your settings and data (`%APPDATA%\Poni`).

The build isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info → Run anyway**.
