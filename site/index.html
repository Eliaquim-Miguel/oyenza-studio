Free and open source (MIT) · Windows 2.4 MB, Mac 10.8 MB · No account needed · What's new in 0.2.0

Oyenza Studio hangs a small island from the top of your screen. It shows what is happening, and opens into quick pages when you point at it: music with synced lyrics, a focus timer, a file shelf your phone can drop into, tasks, your clipboard and, on Windows, money in and bills due from Oyenza.

While it rests

It shows what's happening.

Small and quiet at the top edge. It grows only when something needs a moment of your attention, then gets out of the way.

  1. Album art and a beat. The title peeks in when the song changes.

  2. The countdown keeps going in the island while you work.

  3. A clean pop-up when you change the volume or plug in.

  4. Bluetooth headsets say hello, with their battery level.

  5. With the Prayer times add-on, the island counts down the ten minutes before each prayer.

  6. With Oyenza, the island tells you when money arrives. The amount and sender stay hidden until you click Show.

  7. A clock, a pair of eyes, a blank notch, or nothing until you point.

Point at it

Eight quick pages.
One hover away.

Point at the island or click it and it opens; move away and it closes at once. Scroll over the header to switch pages; the gear on the right opens Settings. Sign in to Oyenza and a ninth page joins them.

Six more pages

The rest of your day, one hover away.

A timer, your steps, a shelf for files, a to-do list, your clipboard and today. Each one is a page of the island, a scroll away from the next.

Focus

A Pomodoro timer that keeps counting in the island.

Health

Today's steps against your goal, and your week, from Apple Health or Health Connect on your phone through Oyenza. Signing in works now; the steps come in a later version.

Shelf

Drop files on the island to keep them handy, or send them from your phone. Click to open, or drag them back out.

Tasks

A tiny to-do list for the things you'd otherwise forget.

Clipboard

Recent copied text, kept in memory only. It skips anything a password manager marks as private.

New in 0.1.18 · Peeks instead of staying in 0.1.20

Show over games. FPS, GPU and CPU.

While a game fills the screen in borderless full-screen, the island becomes a small readout: frames per second, the graphics card's load and temperature, and the CPU's load. It shows for a few seconds when the game comes to the front, when the frame rate drops sharply, and whenever you press Alt+Shift+F, then gets out of the way.

Frames are counted the way Intel's PresentMon and Xbox Game Bar count them, from Windows' own log of each frame a game shows. Nothing is loaded into the game, so anti-cheat has nothing to see.

Alt+Shift+F

Switch it on in System › Games, or right-click the island › In full-screen apps › Show FPS, GPU and CPU over games. DirectX 9 to 12, Vulkan and OpenGL.

New in 0.1.13

Stays on top.

When another window covers the island, such as a browser left on top after full screen or an app set to stay on top, the island goes back on top.

In full-screen games and videos you choose: hide the island, or keep it on top. Right-click the island or open Settings, then In full-screen apps › Hide or Stay on top. Hide is the default. A game in exclusive full-screen mode still covers every window.

Themes

Four looks.
One island.

The island stays black and quiet by default. Change its look any time: open the island and press the gear, or right-click it › Theme.

Classic. Black and quiet, the default look.

Optional theme · New look in 0.1.7

Oyen takes over the island.

Switch on the Oyen theme and the whole island turns Oyen: warm cream, dark brown text, his orange on the controls, and Oyen himself peeking over the edge, the same Oyen as in the Oyenza app.

  • Cream and brown on every page, with Oyen's orange on the play button, the progress and volume bars and the switches.
  • Every Latin letter is set in Outfit, this website's typeface, which now ships inside the app.
  • Built from the Oyenza logo, so he stays sharp at every size and scaling. On an open page he peeks up beside the pin, eyes on your cursor.
  • He moves only when something happens and sleeps after midnight, so he costs no CPU while nothing moves.

Open the island and press the gear, then Theme › Oyen. Switch back to Classic any time.

Optional theme · New in 0.1.13

Glass. The island as liquid glass.

Its edge bends and magnifies what's under it, the middle is frosted, a light runs along the rim, and it turns light or dark with what's behind it.

  • To draw the glass, Oyenza Studio copies the part of the screen behind its window (the island and the space it opens into) while Glass is on, and not while the island is hidden or the PC is locked. The copy stays in your PC's memory, each new copy replaces the last, and it's never saved or sent.
  • While Glass is on, Windows leaves the island out of screenshots, recordings and screen sharing.
  • Since 0.1.16 Glass also works on PCs whose colour settings make Windows draw the desktop in 16-bit colour, and on HDR displays. Where it can't copy the screen (Remote Desktop, Windows 10 before version 2004, some laptops), Glass draws smoked glass instead: the same shape and light over a see-through tint, and it stays in screenshots.
  • Classic stays the default look.

The small island turns light or dark with what's behind it. Open the island and press the gear, then Theme › Glass.

Optional theme · New in 0.1.21

Pill. The island floats.

Pill changes the island's shape, not just its colour. It floats a little below the top of the screen as a black capsule, with layouts of its own: the time and battery while it rests, the song while music plays, and a rounded card when it opens.

  • A soft shadow and a fine rim keep it apart from the wallpaper, light or dark.
  • Colour only where it means something: the album's colour for music, green for a charging battery, red for a low one.
  • Status bar is Pill's own idle look: right-click › Idle look › Status bar. The other themes keep the clock.
  • On a MacBook with a notch, the resting island stays beside the camera, and pop-ups, notices and the open card float just below it: see On a Mac
  • The pages and features are the same as in Classic. Classic stays the default look.

Right-click the island, then Theme › Pill. Or open the island, press the gear and pick Pill under Theme. On a Mac, it's in the menu bar's Theme menu. Switch back to Classic any time.

Oyenza Studio for Mac

On a MacBook with a camera notch, the island lives around it: what's playing sits either side of the camera, and the island opens downward from the notch when you point at it. On a Mac without a notch, or on another display, it hangs from the top of the screen as it does on Windows.

Around the notch, the Oyen theme goes black like the notch itself, with Oyen's cream type, his orange and his face on it.

  • Music and Spotify, with synced lyrics from LRCLIB, or Apple Music's own with your token, and the reading under Japanese, Korean and Chinese lines.
  • The focus timer, the Shelf with Phone drop over the same Wi-Fi, tasks, clipboard history, and the System page with your Mac's CPU, graphics, memory, drives, network, battery and busiest apps.
  • Pop-ups for volume and charging, the Classic, Oyen, Glass and Pill themes, and start at login. Every option is in the menu bar, under Oyen's mark.
  • New in 0.1.19: Oyen goes black around the notch; Glass is macOS's own glass, Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and a dark blur before it, with a black strip where it meets the notch; and the System page reads your Mac, the way Stats shows it.
  • New in 0.1.21: Pill. On a MacBook with a notch, the resting island, the timer and music between lines stay beside the camera; the line being sung, pop-ups, notices and the open island float just below the notch. Without a notch it floats below the top of the screen, as on Windows.

Oyenza account · On Windows · Live view new in 0.1.17

Your Oyenza, at the top of your screen.

Sign in with your oyenza.app account the way you sign in on a TV: press the gear, choose Sign in with Oyenza, and approve the code the island shows at oyenza.app/connect. No password is ever typed into Oyenza Studio. An Oyenza page then joins the island.

  • Your Oyenza, live, by day. Every transaction of the last 7 days, money in and out, newest first. Pick a day on the strip, and what's logged while you look glows.
  • Money in and out as it happens. A notice when Oyenza logs money coming in or going out, with the amount hidden until you click Show.
  • Bills before they're due. Subscriptions and card due dates for the next 30 days, and a reminder the day before each one.
  • Note a spend in a second. Type "kopi 25rb" on the Oyenza page and press Enter: it goes straight to Oyenza, with 10 seconds to undo.
  • Your steps, next. Steps from Apple Health or Health Connect will come through Oyenza in a later version.

Signing in is optional. This PC gets a key of its own, never your password: it sees your last 7 days of transactions and the bills due, and saves the spends you type: never balances, account numbers, notes or receipts. Disconnect it in the island's Settings, or in oyenza.app under Connected apps.

Smooth and light

Install

Ready in under a minute.

Coming from Oyen Poni? Updating keeps your settings and data.

WindowsWindows 10 and 11

  1. Download the installer

    Oyenza-Studio-Setup.exe, from the latest release on GitHub. A portable zip is on the Releases page too.

  2. Run it

    It installs just for you and needs no admin rights.

  3. If Windows warns you

    Builds aren't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC". Choose More info, then Run anyway. If Smart App Control is on, Windows won't run unsigned apps at all; a Microsoft Store version is on the way.

MacmacOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel

  1. Download the app

    Oyenza-Studio-Mac.dmg, from the same release.

  2. Drag it into Applications

    Open the DMG and drag Oyenza Studio into the Applications folder.

  3. Allow it the first time

    It isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS warns you the first time you open it. Click Done, then open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Music

Music, with the words.

Controls for whatever is playing: Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube in your browser, or any app that shows up in Windows' media controls. Lyrics scroll in time with the song, from the free LRCLIB library, or Apple Music's own synced lyrics if you have a subscription.

YouTube videos find theirs too, even when the title carries subtitle tags or puts the song name first. After an intro or a break, the waiting dots fold away the moment the next line starts.

Drag the time bar to jump anywhere in a song: the times follow as you slide, and it jumps when you let go. Since 0.1.20 that works with Apple Music too, and so does Back: the island uses Apple Music's own slider and back button. Lyrics glide into place instead of stepping a pixel at a time.

Read along

Read along in Korean, Japanese and Chinese.

Like Apple Music's Show Pronunciation, each word of a lyric line gets its reading in Latin letters, set small underneath: the syllables for Korean, romaji for Japanese and pinyin with tone marks for Chinese.

Japanese kanji are read with Windows' own reading analyzer. On PCs without Windows' Japanese data, kana lines read right away and the Music page offers Japanese readings, a 3.8 MB add-on that reads every kanji line.

Since 0.1.15 it reads closer to how the words are sung: numbers with their counters (二人 futari, 一歩 ippo), は as "wa" after every word (心には kokoro ni wa), and words like 愛おしい and 失くす. Turn it off in Settings or the tray menu.

New in 0.1.11 · Redone in 0.1.17 · On the Mac in 0.1.19

System: your PC at a glance.

CPU, GPU, memory, drives, network and battery in rings and charts: each core's load with performance and efficiency cores apart, the graphics card's engines and memory, memory pressure and what fills memory, read, write, download and upload speeds, and the processes using the most.

Since 0.1.17 it reads Windows' own kernel counters and keeps the last minute while the page is closed, so the charts are full the moment it opens. Since 0.1.20 that minute rests while your PC is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on. No driver, no admin rights, and nothing leaves your PC.

On a Mac it reads macOS's own counters the way Stats does, and rests while the screen is asleep or the Mac is locked.

Keep the page on your iPhone's Home Screen, or install it on Android, where Oyenza Studio joins the share sheet. The phone and the PC meet through drop.oyenza.com, and the files go straight between them over an encrypted link. Paired phones lists each phone with Remove.

Prayer times, for your kab/kota.

Today's times as Kemenag RI publishes them for your kab/kota, the next prayer and how long until it, and the qibla. Windows' location finds the nearest of Kemenag's 517 places and checks again when your laptop travels, or you type your city. The point stays on your PC: it only picks the place.

New in 0.1.18 · Peeks instead of staying in 0.1.20

Show over games: FPS, GPU and CPU.

While a game fills the screen in borderless full-screen, the island becomes a small readout: frames per second, the graphics card's load and temperature, and the CPU's load. It shows for a few seconds when the game comes to the front, when the frame rate drops sharply, and whenever you press Alt+Shift+F, then gets out of the way: any window over a borderless game makes Windows combine the two before they reach the screen, which costs about a frame, so with the island gone the game's frames go straight to the screen again. Switch it on in System › Games, or right-click the island › In full-screen apps › Show FPS, GPU and CPU over games.

DirectX 9 to 12, Vulkan and OpenGL. The first time, Windows asks once for an admin OK, as Game Bar's FPS counter does, and FPS shows from your next sign-in. Over a full-screen app, Glass draws smoked glass instead of copying the screen.
